## The week in one paragraph
This was the big earnings week, and the answer was **more**. The 10-Qs (Tier 1) show Q3 cash capex of **$77.3B at Amazon** (up from $54.2B in Q2), $51.4B at Alphabet and $39.4B at Microsoft. Add Meta's $33.9B and Oracle's $28.5B and the five spend about **$230B a quarter**. Nobody cut. Amazon kept about $220B for 2026 and reportedly said 2027 will be "meaningfully more." Alphabet reportedly pointed to more than $300B in 2027, but that rests on one source. Demand is real: AWS grew 39% and Azure grew 48%, both faster than last quarter. But the build now costs more than these companies make. Amazon's trailing free cash flow fell to about **negative $42B**, so two of the big four are now spending past their cash flow. Anthropic filed its public S-1 on Oct 26 (proposed ticker ANT), which confirms the H1 figures we had held as contested. Broad credit markets calmed: the high-yield spread eased to 3.26%.

## Money in vs money out
Latest actual quarterly capex (SEC, Tier 1): **Amazon $77.3B, Alphabet $51.4B, Microsoft $39.4B, Meta $33.9B** (all September quarter) and Oracle $28.5B (August quarter). That totals about **$230B per quarter, up from about $197B last week.** Revenue signals: AWS has a $184B run-rate growing 39%, Azure is growing 48%, and Google Cloud reportedly grew 103% (single source). Cash flow tells the other side. Amazon's trailing FCF is −$42.4B, down from +$14.8B a year ago, and Meta's Q3 FCF was −$2.1B. Revenue is accelerating, but capex is growing faster.
